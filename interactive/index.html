Multi-omics of Breast Cancer: change the settings, watch the results move

Every figure here is drawn from the real TCGA-BRCA results of the four notebooks. Move a slider or pick a gene and the counts, statistics and plots are recomputed in your browser. The starting position of every control is the setting used in the notebooks, so you always begin at the published answer.

NOTEBOOK 1

Quality control

Sample QC: where do you draw the line?

975 RNA-seq samples (863 tumours, 112 normals). Each rule removes samples below or above a cut-off. The right-hand plot is the outlier check: how similar each sample is to the others of its own type.

Try this
  • Raise the minimum library size until samples start to fail. How strict must the rule be before any sample is lost?
  • Lower the mitochondrial cut-off to 10 %. Are these samples outliers in the right-hand plot, or ordinary tumours?
  • Set the outlier rule to k = 1.5 (the usual box-plot rule). How many “outliers” now – and would you really remove them all?

Sex check: one gene or four?

Y-chromosome genes are expressed only in male cells. A sample whose Y-gene level disagrees with the recorded gender points to a sample swap. Black crosses = disagreement.

Try this
  • Switch the score from “mean of 4 genes” to RPS4Y1 alone. How many recorded women now look male?
  • Try each single gene. Which one gives the cleanest split, and why is a mean of several genes safer?

Removing barely expressed genes

19,944 protein-coding genes. A gene is kept if its CPM reaches a cut-off in at least a chosen share of samples. Removed genes (grey) sit at the low end of expression.

Try this
  • Change 50 % to 10 % (the “Your turn” question in Notebook 1). How many genes are kept?
  • Raise the CPM cut-off from 1 to 10. Which part of the left histogram disappears?

PCA of all samples

Each dot is a sample; nearby dots have similar expression profiles. Notebook 1 used the 2,000 most variable genes.

Try this
  • Colour by PAM50 subtype. Which subtype forms its own group along PC2?
  • Colour by mitochondrial reads. Do the four flagged tumours sit apart from the rest?
  • Change 2,000 genes to 500 or 5,000. Does the picture change much?

Protein table: drop or impute?

487 proteins × 854 tumours (RPPA). Missing values come in blocks: some antibodies were used on only part of the samples, and some samples were run with a smaller panel.

Try this
  • Relax the sample rule to 60 %. How many extra samples, and how many extra values must be imputed?
  • Allow proteins with up to 100 % missing. What happens to the number of imputed values?
NOTEBOOK 2

Tumour vs the patient's own normal tissue

p-values and multiple testing

13,774 genes, one paired t-test each. The dashed line is the height every bar would have if no gene changed. Compare raw p-values, Benjamini–Hochberg FDR and Bonferroni.

Try this
  • Choose “Simulation: no gene changes”. How many genes pass p < 0.05, and how many survive FDR?
  • Back on the real data: why is the left-most bar so tall?
  • Compare the paired and the unpaired test: which gives more extreme p-values?

Volcano plot with your own cut-offs

Each dot is a gene (or miRNA): x = how much it changes, y = how sure we are. Switch between the paired t-test, the PyDESeq2 count model, the unpaired test and the miRNA data.

Try this
  • Set the fold-change cut-off to 0. How many genes are “significant” now, and are they all important?
  • Switch to PyDESeq2 at the default cut-offs. How many more genes, and which side gains most?
  • Search ESR1, ERBB2, MKI67. Switch the plot to MA plot: do big changes happen only for low-expressed genes?
  • Choose miRNA and search mir-21 and mir-486-1.

One gene, 111 patients

Each line joins one patient's normal tissue to the same patient's tumour. Type any of the 13,774 tested genes.

Try this
  • Compare COL10A1 (up) with FABP4 (down). Do nearly all patients change the same way?
  • Look at GAPDH, a “housekeeping” gene. Is it really constant?
  • Find a gene that goes up in some patients and down in others.

From gene lists to biology: test your own gene set

Does a whole group of genes shift together? The Mann–Whitney test compares the t-statistics of the set with all other genes. Load one of the Notebook 2 themes or type your own list.

Try this
  • Load the four themes in turn and note the medians. Which go up, which go down – and which of these are about cell composition?
  • Load Immune cells and Housekeeping. Type a random list of 10 genes: what p-value do you get?
NOTEBOOK 3

Do the layers tell the same story?

How well does mRNA predict protein?

836 tumours with both layers; 204 genes have a matching antibody. Pick a pair; untick subtypes to see the correlation within groups.

Try this
  • ER (ESR1 ↔ ERALPHA): keep only Luminal A and Luminal B. What happens to r?
  • Scroll to the bottom of the list: what do the weakest pairs look like?

miRNAs and their target genes

A miRNA that silences a gene should correlate negatively with it across tumours. The list below the plot ranks genes by correlation with the chosen miRNA (click one to plot it).

Try this
  • Click through the four Notebook 3 examples. Which one is positive, and why?
  • For miR-155, look at the most positive genes in the list. What kind of cell makes them?
  • miR-18a ↔ ESR1: untick Basal-like. Does the strong negative correlation survive?

Each layer alone, and all three integrated

PCA of the tumours from one layer or from all three combined. “Neighbour agreement” measures how well a layer groups tumours of the same subtype.

Try this
  • Compare the neighbour agreement of the four views. Does integration beat mRNA alone?
  • Integrated view: switch block weighting off, then use 5,000 mRNA genes. Which layer now dominates?

Subtype markers in each layer

The features that differ most between the four subtypes (F-test), each row scaled to mean 0 (blue low, red high), tumours grouped by subtype.

Try this
  • Look at the top 5 in each layer. Do mRNA, miRNA and protein point to the same biology for Basal-like tumours?
NOTEBOOK 4

Machine learning

Predicting the PAM50 subtype: features (k) and regularisation (C)

Logistic regression in a pipeline (scale → keep the k best features → fit with strength C). 477 training and 160 test tumours. Every setting was really fitted and scored.

Try this
  • Set C = 10 (almost no shrinkage) with k = all. Compare training and test accuracy.
  • Protein with k = 5: how much accuracy do you keep with only five antibodies?
  • Which layer is least sensitive to the settings?

ER status by the pathologist: ROC curve and threshold

Predicting a label measured independently (immunohistochemistry). Move the threshold and watch the point travel along the ROC curve.

Try this
  • Find a threshold with no false negatives. What does it cost in false positives?
  • Look at the confident disagreements: is the model wrong, or could the stain be?

Leakage simulator: accuracy from pure noise

The labels were shuffled, so the honest answer is chance (0.25). Each dot is one repeat with a different random study; the line is the mean of 10 repeats.

Try this
  • Make the study small (50 tumours) and select few genes. How good does the wrong procedure look?
  • Increase to 400 tumours. Does the leak go away?

Bonus: tumours without a label

183 tumours had no PAM50 call. The final all-layer model predicts one; how many predictions should you trust?

Try this
  • Raise the threshold to 0.95. Which subtype loses the most calls?
